# Method notes

## Is this different from the scientific method?

No. It *is* the scientific method — conjecture, predict, test, revise. Nothing
here is a new epistemology and it would be silly to dress it up as one.

What *is* worth writing down is that computational/mathematical research inverts
one prior badly, and that inversion drives most of the technique below.

**In empirical science, a surprising result might be a discovery. In
computational research on your own code, a surprising result is almost always
your own bug.** Nature is not being subtle; you made a mistake. Every anomaly
starts guilty.

Three further differences from lab science shape everything:

1. **Experiments are exact and deterministic.** No noise, no statistics, no
   p-values. `other = 0 in 7/7` is categorical. Falsification is sharp — and a
   single counterexample is fatal rather than "anomalous".
2. **Ground truth is usually independently computable.** The same quantity can
   often be obtained by a completely unrelated route. That is far stronger than
   more tests of the same code.
3. **Empirical regularities can be promoted to theorems.** This is the real
   difference from lab science. If you see an invariance, try to *prove* it —
   and the proof usually comes from reading the construction, not from more
   measurements.

## The loop, as actually run here

```
   IDEATE: a slate of candidates from several generators, ranked by what
        |  each would change for the goal (see "Where ideas come from")
        v
   observe something odd
        |
        v
   [BUG CHECK]  <-- assume it is your fault; discharge this FIRST
        |
        v
   conjecture a mechanism          <-- a mechanism, not a matching number
        |
        v
   DERIVE from the construction: a consequence you have NOT yet measured,
   an obstruction, or "not reached"
        |
        v
   [DESIGN REVIEW]  <-- before any compute: is every cited result about this
        |               object? what else would explain each outcome? what
        |               happens next under each outcome?
        v
   test only what the derivation could not settle
        |
    +---+---+
    |       |
  fails   holds
    |       |
    v       v
 retract  try to promote to a proof (read the construction)
 + log      |
            v
     state scope limits, then move on
```

The steps people skip are the bug check, *deriving before measuring*, and
reviewing the design before running it. All three earned their place here
expensively; the third was added on 2026-09-16, after a day of compute went
into testing a coincidence that a derivation could not have supported.

## Where ideas come from

Most of the backlog has grown depth-first: an item's outcome names the
residual that becomes the next item (TODOs 26→27→28→29, 35→36→37,
47→48→49→50). That is good at finishing a thread and bad at noticing that a
different thread matters more, and several of those chains ended in "matched
timings lose" or "novelty excluded". The two results that changed the project's
direction most came from elsewhere. C15's proof came from noticing a conjugate
of an involution in the construction; C99 came from reading Pauli paths as
differential characteristics. Neither was a residual.

So before committing to a direction, build a **slate**. Generators:

1. **Structure inventory.** List every structure the actual construction
   carries (the group generated by the blocks, GF(2)-affine maps and frames,
   2-adic orders, involutions and conjugacies, Walsh duality, recurrences,
   planarity) and put the standard questions to each: orbits, stabilizers and
   presentations; rank and affine closure; the growth base of a recurrence;
   degree, influence and spectral norm; automaton or decision-diagram width.
2. **Transfer by dictionary.** Name the object in an adjacent field that has
   theorems, then import the theorem or its obstruction. Fields that have paid
   or are one step away here: Fourier analysis of Boolean functions, linear and
   differential cryptanalysis, branching-program and communication-complexity
   lower bounds, automata and Nerode classes, combinatorics on words.
3. **Obstruction first.** Ask which known lower bound or hardness result would
   make the goal impossible in this direction, then check its hypotheses
   against our regime in the source body. A direction that has survived its
   strongest known obstruction is worth more than one never checked, and a
   theorem whose hypotheses we escape says exactly where to look.
4. **Exact sequences to closed forms.** Exact integer counts at consecutive
   sizes are data for guessing, not for curve fitting: look for a linear
   recurrence, a rational generating function or a known integer sequence,
   then derive it. A guessed recurrence predicts the next term exactly; a
   log-fit on four points does not (the DD growth fit of 2026-09-16 could not
   separate λ^t from t^p).
5. **Symmetry search at tiny sizes.** Enumerate transformations of the
   construction (a base to its inverse, variable reversal, complement,
   moduli with equal order) where every case is cheap, and record exact
   invariances before designing comparisons. The inverse-base symmetry of
   2026-09-16 was found only after it had confounded a test.
6. **Ablation and free models.** Replace a component by a random one that
   satisfies the same stated relations (C103's free model), or remove it.
   The ablation that kills the effect names what carries it.
7. **Change the question.** Final vs peak, nodes vs bytes, expectation vs
   sampling, clean code vs full space. The same construction often has an easy
   and a hard version, and knowing which is which is a result.

**Choosing from the slate.** One line each: the goal it serves, what is true if
it succeeds, its strongest known obstruction, and the cheapest step whose
outcome would change the ranking. Keep at least three candidates from at least
two generators alive until one has survived its obstruction check and its
cheapest step. Record killed candidates with the reason, as with retractions.
When one thread has produced three residual items in a row, build a slate
before opening a fourth.

## Exploration is not a test

Measuring without a derivation is allowed, and it is how most targets here
were found: CNOT being memory-neutral in Heisenberg form, the turn in the
DD-native ratio. Call it **exploration**. Keep it cheap. It can suggest a
conjecture and it can never confirm one: a result with no derivation behind it
is an observation, and the next step is the derive box, not another run.

- **A numerical match is not a mechanism.** "The ratio turns at n_exp = 4, and
  α + μ(β) + 2 = 4 on that instance" is one coincidence. The derivation has to
  say why that quantity governs this object. If it cannot, the result is "not
  reached", and the match does not license a test.
- **A cited result must be about the same object.** Quote the object sentence
  of the cited claim and compare it with yours. A bound on the ROBDD of the
  final function says nothing by itself about a diagram of Walsh coefficients
  in the middle of a propagation.
- **Every run states its decision value before it starts:** what will be
  derived, built or dropped under each outcome. If every outcome leads to the
  same next step, do not run it.

## Techniques that paid for themselves

**Prior art first.** Cheap, decisive, and it can invalidate everything
downstream. Ran it as step 1 before any writing. It narrowed the novelty claim
substantially and cost hours instead of a referee report.

**Derive-then-test.** Predict a consequence *before* looking. Used twice:
branch symmetry `|ĥ|=|ĝ|`, and support confinement `z_I ∈ {0,1_I}`. Both
confirmed with zero violations. Evidence from a prediction is worth far more
than an explanation fitted to data already seen — and it is the only way to
avoid dressing up a curve fit as a mechanism.

**Every experiment carries a control that must FAIL.** The one rule it is most
tempting to skip once a hypothesis is going well, and it has now caught two
vacuous measurements of mine that had already produced confident-looking
numbers:

- *step 9* — synthetic blocks acted only on b-qubits while the observable was
  `Z_x0`, so both block types came out constant for a trivial reason;
- *TODO 12e* — the tail-confinement test `z_I ∈ {0, 1_I}` is vacuous at
  |I| = 1, because one bit *is* all-zeros or all-ones. The β>1 control passed
  when it had to fail, which is the only reason it was noticed — and three
  rows of the positive result were being read as evidence too.

In both cases nothing measured was wrong. What was wrong was how much the
measurement was entitled to say, and only the control could tell the
difference. `lab.harness` warns when no must-fail control is registered.

(The project's other two self-caught errors came from different rules, not
this one: "vary exactly one parameter" caught a sweep that drew a new random
table per t, and null-model hygiene caught random tables degenerating to
constants at small r. Worth keeping straight — the rules are not
interchangeable.)

**Precision sweep to separate bug from float error.** Re-run in `longdouble`.
If the error is *identical*, it is a logic bug, full stop. This caught the θ=π
bug that six test suites had missed.

**An independent exact reference beats more tests.** The Walsh transform
computed the same quantity by a completely different route and immediately
exposed a propagator bug that every existing suite passed. Build the second
route early.

**Vary exactly one parameter.** Fix the modulus, vary the base. An
ancilla-count confound (10 qubits vs 15) had previously killed an entire thesis
that looked well-supported.

**Step by 1, not 2.** The `n_exp = v₂(r)+1` threshold was invisible while
stepping n_exp in twos — and the original sweep happened to *start* exactly on
the threshold, so the claim looked stronger than it was. Pure luck, in the
dangerous direction.

**Randomised tests only cover what they sample.** The random-circuit suite drew
from `{h,t,cnot,rx,rz}` and so never emitted an `X`. Enumerate the gate *set*;
do not sample it and call it coverage.

**Check that the measured quantity is the quantity that matters.** "Exact cost
model" predicted `N_final` while PPS memory is `N_max`. The identity was right;
the claim was about the wrong number.

**Read the construction when experiments stall.** Two experimental routes to the
C15 proof failed. The proof came from reading four lines of the circuit
definition and noticing `u_a(·,1) = A⁻¹SA` — a conjugate of an involution. No
measurement would have produced that.

## Failure modes seen here, with names

| failure | instance |
|---|---|
| **Right conclusion, wrong mechanism** | adder collapses to 1 term — because permutation (wrong) vs because affine (right). Still counts as a failure. |
| **Toy that isn't a proxy** | H → adder → QFT "Shor sandwich": the observable barely met the arithmetic. |
| **Confound in a matched comparison** | compilation A/B with mismatched qubit counts. |
| **Bug agreeing with a hypothesis** | the θ=π bug produced numbers consistent with the compilation thesis, which is why it survived. |
| **Lucky sampling hiding a threshold** | every sweep started at n_exp = α+1. |
| **Trusting a summary over source** | web summaries claimed Qiskit `pauli-prop` supports Toffoli; the source rejects it. |
| **Reading an abstract, not the body** | Dang et al.'s abstract says "factors of r"; §4 is explicitly 2-adic. Nearly caused a wrong retraction. |
| **A test with only one possible answer** | `z_I ∈ {0, 1_I}` at \|I\| = 1. Caught by the must-fail control, which passed. See §OS3. And again a day later: a sign test on monotone data, where every pair "agrees" for free (§DF, P3). Both returned unanimous passes, which is the tell. |
| **Testing a coincidence as a mechanism** | the DD-native PPS peak ratio turned at n_exp = 4 for N = 7, a = 3, where C103's α + μ(β) + 2 is also 4. Five families of compute later the order came out reversed. A derivation would have had to explain why a bound on the final function's ROBDD governs a diagram built during propagation, and could not have. |
| **Confound visible from arithmetic alone** | the "α-replicate" bases a = 4 and a = 5 at N = 7 are the inverses of a = 2 and a = 3, and at N = 7 every same-order pair is an inverse pair, so the replication could not separate α from inversion symmetry. Asking "what else explains a match?" before running finds this with no compute. |
| **Correction by patch** | a defect is fixed where the referee pointed and nowhere else. C100's v2 added a wrong justification to an already proven claim; TODO 55's v2 marked a refuted control fixed and repeated its class in a different control. Read what shares the corrected sentence's derivation. |
| **A referee's formula used as a source** | the alpha + mu crossover went from an *accepted* review into a canonical claim verbatim and was wrong by one level: D_mu(mu) = 2^mu exactly, so the bound is trivial for every k <= alpha + mu. A review is a correction list to verify, not a citable derivation. |
| **A tolerance tightened between versions** | a +/-3 KB precision band became "exactly 4S" in the next version, which array headers make unreachable. Tightening a measured band without re-deriving the exact value, overheads included, converts a passing reading into an impossible one. |
| **A control that fails to fail because the *predicate* is wrong** | §DF's β=1 control demanded monotone growth across the whole sweep, but the support only locks at n_exp = α+1, so D legitimately drops once first. The control was right; the code asking it was not. Fixing it produced a *sharper* control (|S| exactly constant past the lock). |

## Bookkeeping that mattered

- **A retraction log is worth more than a results list.** Half of what was
  believed at various points is now marked dead. Without the log, a future
  session re-derives dead ends.
- **Write for a reader with no context** — including yourself in a later
  session. `NOTES.md` is context restoration, not a report.
- **Mark stale files in the file itself**, not only in the index. Retracted
  experiments carry warning headers because an index entry will be missed.
- **Record *why* something was believed**, not just that it was wrong. "Killed
  by the θ=π bug" is more useful than "wrong".
