## 1. Paper A's universal peak formula is false

Location: Paper A §5, Proposition 2; claim C44.

A four-qubit circuit containing only these two gates, in forward order,
is a counterexample with observable Z on qubit 2:

```python
qc = Circuit(4)
qc.toffoli(3, 1, 0)
qc.toffoli(1, 0, 2)
```

Atomic propagation peaks at **4** terms. Rotation-level propagation peaks
at **10** terms. A dense 16-by-16 matrix calculation independently confirms
the rotation peak of 10. Final Walsh and atomic-propagation coefficients agree
exactly. The formula in Proposition 2 predicts 6 for the peak sets here;
even its weaker implied upper bound of 2 × 4 = 8 is violated.

The proof tracks rotations within the target's X/Y plane but does not track
the changes to the rest of the Pauli label induced by intervening CNOTs and
the subsequent interference. That local argument does not establish the
claimed relation between global maxima.

This does not refute the measured arithmetic rows. It refutes the purported
general derivation. Keep the arithmetic relation as a measured regularity,
or prove it with explicit additional hypotheses on the incoming operator and
the circuit family. The exact atomic-peak characterization as the maximum
Walsh sparsity over suffixes survives.

## 2. The supplied compilations do not implement the same full-space permutation

Locations: Paper A abstract, §§3.2 and 4.1.

The theorem's invariance under equal full-space permutations is correct.
The claim that these two supplied implementations demonstrate equal final
cost is incorrect. For N=5, a=2, n_exp=2, measuring x[0]:

| Implementation | Qubits | Final support |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Fourier | 10 | 451 |
| Toffoli | 15 | 15,493 |

I reproduced both support counts. This distinction is already explained in
`notes/status-critical-bug-found-all-retracted.md`: the circuits agree on the
valid subspace but have different scratch layouts and full-space actions.
Adding unused identity ancillas would preserve the Fourier support count,
so matching dimensions by padding does not establish equal permutations.

Also, the cited six-case `experiment_c8.py` verifies four Toffoli-modexp
observables and two ripple-adder observables; it does not contain a Fourier
comparison. Z-closure in both implementations and equality of their
full-space pullbacks are different claims.

For a clean compilation-invariance demonstration, take one fixed logical
permutation and compare two verified decompositions of that same operation
on the same register. Describe the current arithmetic examples as independent
applications of the identity, with different full-space functions.

## 3. Odd order factors do not force every output bit to be Walsh-dense

Location: Paper B §4 and the function-level dichotomy framing.

For N=13, a=4, the order is r=6=3×2. The orbit is

```text
1, 4, 3, 12, 9, 10
```

Its least significant bit is `1,0,1,0,1,0`, so the selected bit function is
`g(e) = 1 XOR e_0`. It has Walsh sparsity **1**, despite beta=3.
The saved probe confirms this at exponent widths 4, 8, and 12.

The relevant period at function level is the minimal period of the selected
scalar output function, which can be a proper divisor of the multiplicative
order. A constant-output-bit exclusion alone does not fix this example.

This counterexample concerns the idealized scalar function, not the measured
full-space circuit supports. The circuit-level beta=1 theorem remains valid.
The beta>1 branch needs its empirical status and observable assumptions kept
explicit. Failure of the parity proof's hypotheses is not a proof of density.

Similarly, the parity proof establishes constancy once a nonempty identity
tail is present. Proving that the *first* lock must occur at alpha+1 requires
an additional argument excluding earlier stabilization for the chosen
observable. The measured onset rows do not supply that universal lower bound.

## 4. The useful-support fraction is not exactly 2^-(alpha+1)

Location: Paper B §10.2; claim C42 and note BI.

The claim that any term carrying Z on an initially |+> exponent qubit has
zero expectation is exact. Equal cardinalities of the permitted spectral
sectors do not follow from parity confinement.

Reproduced counts:

| Instance | Useful / total | Fraction | Claimed exact fraction |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| N=7, a=6, t=2 or 3 | 3883 / 15549 | 0.2497266705 | 0.25 |
| N=5, a=2, t=3 | 4014 / 32143 | 0.1248794450 | 0.125 |

These are already the notebook's counts. In fact, an odd total of 15,549
cannot have exactly one quarter of its support in any sector. Retain the
exact width invariance and exact characterization of contributing terms;
label the quarter/eighth proportions approximate measurements.

## 5. Novelty and references need another pass

Montanaro and Osborne's *Quantum Boolean Functions*, Proposition 9, explicitly
identifies the Pauli expansion of a diagonal Boolean operator with its
classical Fourier expansion. This is a particularly close citation for
Paper A, beyond the existing Welch citation. The permutation pullback follows
immediately by applying that result to the selected output function.
[Primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/0810.2435#S5).

This does not establish that the arithmetic applications are already published.
It does mean that the strongest novelty case should rest on those applications,
the structural results, and useful simulation consequences, rather than the
identity in isolation.

The paper attributed to “Vidal & Ballarin et al.” is actually by **Timothy N.
Georges, Bjorn K. Berntson, Christoph Sünderhauf, and Aleksei V. Ivanov**:
*Pauli decomposition via the fast Walsh–Hadamard transform*, New Journal of
Physics 27, 033004 (2025), DOI 10.1088/1367-2630/adb44d.
[Primary source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.06206).

I would also replace claims such as “no available implementation” with a
dated, version-specific account of the implementations actually inspected.
A search that did not find a feature is not a universal absence result.

## Other changes that would strengthen the presentation

- **Name the exact resource.** Final support, maximum retained support,
  temporary dictionary allocations, bytes, and runtime are distinct.
  The existing propagators count terms after cancellation/filtering while
  both old and new dictionaries can be resident. Fixed term count also does
  not mean constant runtime as the number of gates grows, or constant bytes
  when integer Pauli masks acquire more bits.
- **Include circuit-evaluation cost.** For a circuit with L constant-size
  logical gates, the implemented replay plus transform costs
  O((L+n) 2^n), not just O(n 2^n). The latter is the FWHT cost given the truth
  table. Extracting a permutation from an arbitrary compiled unitary needs
  a separate assumption or cost analysis. “Exact transform characterization”
  describes the contribution more precisely than “closed-form cost.”
- **Separate the Shor task.** The pullback of a work-register Z before the
  inverse QFT is not a proven lower bound on the expansion of the observable
  produced by an actual Shor output measurement. For diagonal observables
  and an efficiently samplable input basis distribution, ordinary classical
  sampling of the reversible function already estimates the expectation to
  additive accuracy. For a full-space permutation and uniform |+> input,
  the expectation of any nonidentity Z string is exactly zero by trace
  preservation. The representation can still be expensive.
- **Use three size parameters.** Distinguish modulus bit length, exponent
  width, and total physical qubits. The fixed-modulus exponent sweeps and
  varying-modulus 30-qubit series answer different asymptotic questions.
- **Describe the base control accurately.** Varying a preserves the arithmetic
  template and layout but also changes loaded constants and, here, emitted
  gates. It does not literally change only r. Include different bases with
  the same order if making claims beyond the proved identity-tail condition.
- **Keep truncation claims empirical where appropriate.** Terminal magnitude
  thresholding inherits the proved spectrum invariance. Equality of incremental
  peaks at four tested thresholds is not a theorem at every threshold. Measure
  operator error or errors across input states, rather than judging truncation
  quality mainly from one computational-basis expectation.
- **Correct a Boolean-function definition.** Affine support alone does not
  characterize partially bent functions; spectral flatness is also relevant.
  OR on three variables has full, hence affine, Walsh support but unequal
  coefficient magnitudes (3/4 and 1/4).
- **Make setup match the documentation.** The README describes NumPy as the
  only requirement and CUDA as optional, while `pyproject.toml` requires CUDA
  and several other packages. It requires Python >=3.14 while the research
  instructions recommend 3.12/3.13 for long jobs. A minimal CPU environment
  and an explicit supported long-job command would help reproducibility.

## Research angles I would prioritize

### A. Exact elimination of exponent controls after their last use

The existing objection to pruning is correct *inside* a block: control support
can disappear later. But after completing a block, a fresh exponent control
that appears in no earlier arithmetic gate can be contracted with its input
state safely. For a |+> control and work observable O this replaces controlled-V
propagation by

```text
E_V(O) = (O + V† O V) / 2.
```

For V²=I, this averaging map is idempotent: E_V²=E_V. Repeated identity-tail
controls therefore collapse to a single averaging operation for this reduced
observable task. This provides a constructive counterpart to the parity theorem.

The saved probe verifies idempotence with zero error on the actual N=7
identity-tail block; averaging changes 4,096 entries of the tested observable,
so the check is nonvacuous. An order-three cycle gives idempotence error 0.5.

Next measure retained terms, temporary memory, and runtime for block-boundary
contraction versus uncontracted PPS. No new propagator is needed for the proof.
This argument assumes the relevant control is finished and the observable has
the stated form; it does not solve the inverse-QFT output problem.

### B. Turn the window-control criterion into a quantitative theorem

Suppose each disjoint w-bit window controls the same involution via a Boolean
function phi. Set psi=(-1)^phi. The pulled-back sign function has the form

```text
H(y,w_1,...,w_m) = A_+(y) + A_-(y) product_i psi(w_i),
```

where A_+ and A_- are the half-sum and half-difference of the two branches.
This follows directly from the parity of the activated involutions.

If s is the Walsh sparsity of psi, tensor-product factorization controls the
growth. For nonconstant affine phi, s=1 and support remains bounded. For OR on
w>=2 bits, s=2^w. When A_- is nonzero, all nonzero tail frequencies give an
exponential contribution; cancellation with A_+ is confined to the zero-tail
sector. This explains the rate and possible additive offsets, and states the
observable-liveness assumption explicitly.

This would be a stronger presentation than extending “involutive versus not”
or “affine versus nonlinear” from a handful of constructions without a formula.

### C. Compare the same task and scaling limit across methods

The MPS reference really does use the same 2-adic decomposition. Its beta²
saving, however, concerns tensors in a particular partition after changing
their ordering; it is not the same assertion as PPS exponential growth with
exponent width. [Dang, Hill and Hollenberg, §V.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1712.07311v2#S5.SS2).

For fixed N and r, the ideal pre-QFT order-finding state has an exact MPS
representation with exponent-cut bond dimension at most r: the running residue
modulo r is a finite bond index. Increasing exponent width can therefore cost
linearly many fixed-size tensors even when beta>1. This is a useful contrasting
prediction, not a reason to discard the arithmetic connection.

Prioritize a matched comparison on genuine order-finding output sampling, or
explicitly on the same pre-QFT observable. A third simulator exhibiting a
correlation would broaden the evidence but would not prove universality.

For the inverse-QFT direction, the general Pauli/FWHT decomposition already
organizes an operator into XOR-displacement sectors A[y XOR d,y], with a Walsh
transform within each sector. Inspect how many such sectors occur in the
relevant pullbacks before building a new representation theory.
[Georges et al., Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.06206v2#S2).

One inexpensive scope extension for Paper A is also available: arbitrary
computational-basis phases cancel in U†DU when D is diagonal. Its identity
therefore extends from permutations to monomial unitaries (permutation plus
basis-dependent phases).
